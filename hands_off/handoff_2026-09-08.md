## Finding 1 — length-weighted radius statistics (RESOLVED, committed)

**Observation.** Across the 9-combination SEG_LEN sweep, raw AdTree volume
varied by 0.33 % while calibrated volume varied by 33.6 %. Geometry was
invariant; calibration was not.

**Cause.** `build_quantile_matched_pairs()` and
`compute_order_calibration_factors()` computed per-order radius statistics with
one cylinder as one sample, regardless of length. Volume is length-weighted
(`Σ π r² L`) and therefore invariant to discretisation; an unweighted quantile
is not. Resampling changes cylinder density as a function of radius (linear
region `L = K·r` → cylinders per metre ∝ 1/r; on the floor `L = MIN`, constant),
so the statistic moves although the tree does not.

**Evidence.** Order-1 median AdTree radius between two SEG_LEN cases:
39 % shift unweighted → 0.15 % length-weighted, while per-order total length
and volume agreed to within 1 %.

**Fix.** Optional length weighting, additive. `RADIUS_STAT_WEIGHTING_LIST`
computes both variants in one run; `"none"` is byte-identical to the previous
behaviour. Weighted rows carry a `-wlen` suffix in the method string and in
`geom_*.txt` filenames, so the two coexist in `volume_results.csv`.

**Result** (full 9-combination grid, threshold 5 mm, variant 999):

| calmethod | unweighted spread | length-weighted spread |
|---|---|---|
| raw AdTree (control) | 0.33 % | 0.33 % |
| `regression-perorder` | 33.58 % | **0.80 %** |
| `calref=min5mm` | 20.70 % | **0.48 %** |

**Limitation, recorded deliberately.** Weighting does not improve every order.
Per-order coefficient ratios move toward 1 for orders 1–5 but away from it for
orders 6–7 (order 7: 1.053 → 1.105). Those orders carry ~3.2 % of branch
volume, so the aggregate is unaffected, but the mechanism is not uniform.

**Committed**, not pushed: `4956a552e95b6e6f964b94861fcac3f065ad37cb`,
`main` is `[ahead 1]` of `origin/main`. **Push is still outstanding.**

| IND03_088 | 8 | 8 | 0 | 0.204 | 0.204 |

**A fixed definitional offset is ruled out** — two of four trees do not support

Neither AdQSM's `Introduction.pdf` nor AdTree's `ReadMe.txt` defines branch
order, which is why this had to be settled empirically.

Incidental findings:

- The apparent split by maximum order is an **observation only** — with four

## SEG_LEN grid — how it was chosen (documented in the .docx, section 5)

- **Lower bound 27.6 mm** — median skeleton edge length after smoothing on
  `B21_S01` (689,836 edges), consistent with the 2.5 cm uniform subsampling
  applied to all clouds. Resampling only discards existing skeleton vertices,
  so a floor below their own spacing removes nothing and duplicates the
  unresampled model under a new tag.
- **Upper bound 69 mm** — above this the floor reaches the raw AdTree trunk
  radius of the thinnest tree in the set (25 cm measured → ~0.139 m raw AdTree
  radius; AdTree overestimates the B21_S01 trunk by ~11 %, 62.2 vs 56.0 cm).
  The clamp reads AdTree's raw radii, **not** measured DBH and not calibrated
  radii — resampling runs before calibration.
- `SEG_LEN_K = 0.2` dropped as implausibly fine. `SEG_LEN_MAX = 0.5` is
  effectively inactive for these trees (upper break at r = 1.00 m at K = 0.5).

**Predictions vs. measured** — worth correcting in the .docx:
- K's effect on cylinder count is small, as predicted: 2.8 % at r5 mm, growing
  to 13.7 % at r20 mm.
- MIN scaling is **weaker than predicted**: expected 65/35 = 1.86×, measured
  53,491/39,284 = **1.36×**. Likely cause: resampling can only cut at existing
  vertices, and at a 27.6 mm median edge length a 35 mm segment spans only one
  or two edges, so quantisation holds the effective length above the requested
  value.

| MIN/K | r5 mm | r10 mm | r15 mm | r20 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35/0.5 | 53 491 | 23 769 | 15 049 | 10 844 |

| 65/0.5 | 39 284 | 16 143 | 9 833 | 6 878 |
| 65/1.0 | 39 003 | 15 862 | 9 553 | 6 598 |

**ANSYS budget is ~8000 elements** (above that, solve time reaches ~30 min).
Only r20 mm combinations fit. Resampling cannot bridge the gap — MIN scales
counts by 1.36× while the pruning threshold scales them by 5.9×.

**Decision: use different models for different purposes.** r5 mm for the volume
comparison (completeness), r20 mm for ANSYS export (element budget). Pruning
from 5 to 20 mm costs ~20 % of branch volume and ~12 % of total, leaves trunk
volume unchanged, and is structurally negligible — a 4 cm branch against a
56 cm trunk is `(4/56)⁴ ≈ 1/38 000` in bending stiffness. Both thresholds are
produced in the same run as separate rows.

## TreeQSM finding this session (separate workstream)

A `pd2min` sweep on `B21_S01` (4 clean reconstructions, `pd1=0.07`,
`pd2max=0.10`, `mincylrad=0.0025`, manual) established:

- **DBH cannot discriminate between TreeQSM models** — 1.6 % spread across the
  whole sweep, all within ~1 % of the measured 56.0 cm. The DBH-matching
  selection strategy used for AdQSM does not transfer to TreeQSM.
- **`pmdist_mean` and `pmdist_branch_mean` must never be used as selectors.**
  Both are minimised by the `mcr=0.05595` model, which is the known-degraded
  one (branch volume 37.5 m³ vs 17.3 m³ for an otherwise identical model). Fat
  cylinders swallow the point cloud and lower the mean point-to-surface
  distance. Cylinder count barely changed (23,356 vs 23,790), so this is not
  count-driven over-fitting — it arrives through radius instead.
- **`pmdist_trunk_mean` is the only usable candidate** — 24.8 % spread,
  monotonically decreasing with `pd2min`, while trunk volume (4.5 %) and DBH
  (1.6 %) stay flat. A metric that moves without dragging the geometry with it.
  But with no destructive reference for beech it cannot be validated.
- **Manual `pd2min` beats AUTO**: AUTO has the *highest* `pmdist_mean` of all
  seven reconstructions (0.0317 vs 0.0264 for manual `pd2min = 0.020`), at
  comparable branch volume (18.85 vs 17.33 m³) and a third fewer cylinders.

**Acceptance criterion, fixed in advance** for the remaining beeches: lowest
`pmdist_trunk_mean` among models whose `branch_m3` is not an outlier within
that tree's own set, and whose `n_cylinders` fits the ANSYS budget.

`plot_param_sweep.py` (new this session) draws this: 8 small-multiple panels,
SERIES joined by a line, OFF_SERIES with distinct markers identified in the
legend, spread annotated in each panel title (matplotlib autoscales, so a flat
1.6 % panel would otherwise look dramatic). No correlation coefficients — at
n = 4 Spearman is saturated at −1.000 and its p-value comes from a t-approximation
invalid below n ≈ 8.

## ANSYS macro bug (found, not yet fixed — Bára is handling it)

`bk.mac` numbers cylinder start keypoints `1..n` and end keypoints
`10000+i`. Above 10,000 cylinders the ranges collide: keypoint 10001 is both
the end of cylinder 1 and the start of cylinder 10001, and ANSYS's `k` command
*redefines* an existing keypoint, dragging the already-created line's endpoint
across the tree. At 15,049 cylinders that is 5,049 corrupted lines — visible as
long straight lines crossing the crown while the trunk stays clean.

The exported `geom_*.txt` was verified structurally sound: child-start vs
parent-end gaps have median 0.000000 m and max 0.0045 m, all direction vectors
are unit length, no zero-length cylinders, valid parent chain, and the longest
element is 0.204 m — far too short to span the 12.5 m crown.

Fix: replace the hardcoded `10000` with a parameter derived from `n_rad`
(e.g. `kp_off = n_rad + 1000`) at lines 99, 103, 109, 185 and 227. Lines 185
and 227 need care — 227 numbers constraint equations (`ce`), not keypoints, and
185 already carries Bára's own `nutno opravit!` comment.

**Consequence:** models under 10,000 cylinders are unaffected. But the ~8000
element budget was measured in the regime where the macro worked, so once fixed
the real limit above 10,000 can be measured honestly for the first time.
